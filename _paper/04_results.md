# 4. Results

All economic quantities in this section are read from full-physics OPM Flow runs. Surrogate
predictions appear only where the surrogate itself is the object of study (§4.1). ΔNPV is measured
against the historical schedule, NPV_8% = 1,773.4 M US$.

## 4.1 The surrogate is accurate globally and unreliable exactly where the decision is made

On 500 independently generated schedules the learned operator reproduces the simulator closely:
field-level relative error 0.369 %, R² = 0.9959, Spearman ρ = 0.998 against the simulator's
ordering, and a top-1 hit rate of 1.0 (Fig. 2a). Inference costs 18.1 µs per candidate against
approximately one minute for the corresponding simulation, a factor of 3.3 × 10⁶.

That accuracy does not survive the transition to the optimiser's own region. On the ten distinct
schedules returned by a multi-start optimiser run on the surrogate, all within 0.03 % of the
baseline injection volume, the rank correlation falls to ρ = −0.21 (permutation p = 0.56;
bootstrap 95 % CI [−0.83, +0.65]) and the top-1 hit rate falls to 0. The surrogate's first choice
is the simulator's tenth of ten (Fig. 2b). Point accuracy degrades far less than ranking does —
mean relative error rises from 0.369 % to 1.44 % — so the failure is one of ordering, not of
calibration.

Fig. 2c identifies the mechanism. Across the shortlist the surrogate overstates the objective by a
median factor of 1.66, but by 10.6 for the candidate it selects; its predicted value correlates
with its own error at r = 0.97, and the candidate it ranks first is the one it overestimates most
(rank 1 of 10 by error). Selecting on the surrogate therefore selects for surrogate error. The
resulting top-1 regret is 1.39 % of the objective, 46.0 M US$ at r = 8 %, and the surrogate
captures only 28.6 % of the available gain.

Two controls locate the cause in the optimiser rather than in top-*k* selection. Ranking the
surrogate's own top-10 drawn from the **random** pool preserves ρ = 0.95 and a top-1 hit rate of
1.0 across a comparable objective spread (5.0 %); ranking the simulator's true top-10 from the
same pool gives ρ = 0.94. The collapse appears only when candidates are generated by optimising
against the surrogate.

> **Leaf claim.** A surrogate accurate enough to screen at scale (ρ = 0.998) is not accurate
> enough to decide (ρ = −0.21). The full-physics adjudication step is a requirement of the
> architecture, not a safety margin.

## 4.2 Agent-guided closed-loop decisions outperform the no-agent control at a lower simulator cost

Over ten repetitions per arm (Fig. 3a, Table 2), the agent team achieves a mean ΔNPV of
+361.3 M US$ (median +356.9, SD 50.7, best +454.7) and the single agent +347.4 M US$
(median +343.3, SD 72.9, best +508.0). One-dimensional uniform scaling reaches +62.6 M US$.

Both agent arms exceed the no-agent control by a wide margin: +298.6 M US$ for the team
(95 % CI [+262.4, +334.9], p = 1.7 × 10⁻⁸) and +284.8 M US$ for the single agent
(95 % CI [+232.7, +337.0], p = 6.0 × 10⁻⁷).

Two factors change together between the agent arms and this control, and the comparison does not
separate them: the presence of an agent, and the expansion of the decision space from one
dimension to twenty-four. The result therefore establishes that the framework produced
substantially better simulator-verified schedules than the prescribed low-dimensional reference,
not that agent reasoning alone accounts for the gain. §5.5 states this boundary explicitly.

The gap is not bought with simulator time. Each agent run consumed 2.7 (team) and 2.6 (single)
full-physics evaluations on average, against 7 for the uniform sweep (Fig. 3b) — a 5.8-fold larger
gain for roughly 40 % of the simulator budget. This is the operationally relevant comparison: in
this setting the cost of a decision is measured in full-physics runs, and the surrogate's role is
precisely to reduce that count.

## 4.3 The closed loop ends above where it started, in every run

Across all 20 runs the final adjudicated result equals or exceeds the first (20/20; Fig. 4), and
no run ended below the no-agent control. The progression is not monotone round by round: in three
of the twenty runs the last adjudicated candidate fell below the run's own best (e.g. 325.1 →
389.6 → 382.0 M US$), so 17 of 20 runs are non-decreasing at every step. We therefore report
**consistent end-to-end improvement**, not monotone improvement; these are different claims and
only the former is supported. The
programmatic feasibility gate rejected candidates in every arm; rejected candidates are excluded
from the scores above, so the reported gains are attained by schedules the simulator itself
confirmed to be executable within the injection tolerance.

We note that an earlier configuration of the same framework, before the evidence-strength
adjudication rule of §3.4 was introduced, degraded across rounds in six of seven runs
(net −139.6 M US$). The end-to-end improvement reported here is therefore a property of the
adjudication rule, not of closed loops in general.

## 4.4 Decomposing the reasoning into seven roles yields no detectable gain in mean performance

The seven-role team exceeds the single-role arm by +13.8 M US$, which the present experiment
cannot resolve: Welch t-test p = 0.63, Mann–Whitney p = 0.52, 95 % CI [−45.2, +72.8],
Cohen's *d* = 0.22 (Table 2). With n = 10 per arm this is a statement about detectability, not an
assertion of equivalence.

The team arm shows lower dispersion across repetitions (SD 50.7 versus 72.9 M US$), but this
difference is likewise not resolvable (Levene p = 0.45; Bartlett p = 0.29) and is reported as
descriptive only. The single-agent arm produced both the best single run of the study
(+508.0 M US$) and the lowest quartile of outcomes.

Taken with §4.2, the ablation localises the source of the gain. The improvement over the no-agent
control is large and highly significant; the additional decomposition of that reasoning into seven
specialised roles is not, at this sample size, separable from run-to-run variability. The
principal contribution therefore arises from **surrogate-accelerated, simulator-adjudicated
closed-loop decision making** rather than from the number of reasoning roles.

## 4.5 The accepted schedules concentrate on one interpretable direction

To ask whether the agents' solutions are structured or merely well-searched, we regressed ΔNPV on
a single scalar summary of the schedule, the front-loading index

  τ = mean(θ_{1..2,·}) − mean(θ_{5..6,·}),

the difference between early- and late-period mean log multipliers. On 275 randomly sampled and
baseline cases — with all agent- and optimiser-generated solutions excluded, so that the
descriptor is not fitted on the solutions it is used to interpret — τ correlates with ΔNPV at
Spearman ρ = +0.714 (Fig. 5).

The accepted agent schedules sit on the positive side of this axis: mean τ = +0.66 for the team
arm and +0.92 for the single-agent arm. A 24-dimensional policy produced by language-model
reasoning therefore compresses, in part, onto one direction that is interpretable in reservoir
terms — injecting earlier rather than later, consistent with sweep efficiency and discounting
acting in the same direction.

This is an association, not an identified causal effect: τ was not intervened upon, and no
cross-field evidence is presented. Its role here is to show that the agent-generated policies are
not unstructured search output, and to give an operator a single readable summary of what the
framework recommends.

## 4.6 Supporting characterisation

The influence matrix underlying the connectivity role was estimated from 471 simulated cases with
a design-matrix condition number of 2.05, well below the flagging threshold of 30. The estimated
injector influence ranks F-1H (8.57) > F-2H (6.22) > F-3H (6.06) > F-4H (1.76).

Target injection rates across the sampled schedules span 227 to 98,510 (a factor of 430), while
realised rates saturate near 20,000 for all four injectors. The nominal control dimensionality
therefore exceeds the effective one, which is the reason the feasibility gate checks saturation
against measured per-well injectivity rather than against the nominal target.
